Why Mahanu instead of another tool?

The tools below are good at what they do. Mahanu exists because we could not find one home with all of these properties:

  1. readable files on a disk you choose;
  2. calm collaboration, offline work, and optional live presence;
  3. history and recovery in ordinary language;
  4. private projects with a small, deliberate public surface;
  5. a network organized around projects rather than personalities; and
  6. open-source clients and server that people can self-host.

Honest trade-offs

  • Obsidian is a polished Markdown notebook. Choose it when personal knowledge work and plugins matter more than built-in multiplayer or a project network. Also note that the app is proprietary.
  • Google Docs and Notion offer quick collaboration without setup. Where the data lives and the format of data is locked into the platform.
  • Discord plus a shared drive is familiar and excellent for conversation. Keep it when chat is central. Mahanu aims to preserve the plans, decisions, and artifacts that otherwise disappear into chat history.
  • Anytype, AFFiNE, and similar local-first workspaces are close in spirit and available sooner. Their database model versus Mahanu's readable-file model make different trade-offs.
  • Are.na is a calm place to collect and connect public references. Choose it for that. Mahanu focuses on producing and versioning shared work you own.
  • Trello and Asana are mature workflow tools. Their tasks usually describe work stored elsewhere. Mahanu aims to make plans and views part of the same owned project.
  • GitHub proves that project-centered collaboration works. Choose it for code. Mahanu tries to bring understandable versions, online and offline collaboration, and ownership to all kinds of learning and making.

Mahanu earns a place only if the combination works reliably.
