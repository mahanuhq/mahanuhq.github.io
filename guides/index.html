Mahanu for builders and learners

Written for people who might use Mahanu. Short answers to common questions are in the FAQ; this page is the overview.

What is Mahanu?

Mahanu is a network for shared projects: a study circle, band, robotics team, app, or drawing practice. Start with an idea, invite people, make a plan, and build.

One idea makes it different: your project lives on your own computer, as open-format files you can open in any app — even if Mahanu disappeared tomorrow. When you want backup or collaboration, Mahanu syncs those files across your devices and your teammates', with versioned snapshots, and gives you good ways to see the work: calendars, checklists, tables.

One rule defines what belongs: a project is for its members to get better at a shared pursuit. This includes science, art, craft, and sport. See Vision.

How it works

  • Every project has a public card — its name, tagline, and purpose, screened and shown in Explore. Everything else is private to your team unless you deliberately publish it.
  • People join by invite (a link or QR code) or ask to join from your card — you decide, and you set what they can do (edit, comment, or read).
  • Publish deliberately. Choose Publish on a document or image, and only that appears on your project's public page. You can always see exactly what's public; nothing leaks by accident.
  • Clone what you like. Found a good project? Take its structure — folders, schedule, checklists — as a starting point, with a "cloned from" credit that travels with it.
  • Everything has a Timeline. Every change by every member is remembered: see who changed what, revisit any earlier version, restore it. A restore is just another entry in the story.
  • An assistant, on your terms. Ask it to draft a plan or summarize what you collected; it proposes edits and waits for your OK. Every approved edit is labeled and undoable, and it follows instructions your team writes in a plain file it can't hide from you.

What you own

On desktop, everything, from the first save: a project is a folder of open-format files on your own disk — no upload required — and if you stop using Mahanu you keep every note, plan, and image in a form any computer can open (If Mahanu disappears tomorrow). Backing it up and syncing across your devices and teammates is a choice you switch on, not a precondition for the files existing. (A browser-only project is server-backed, because a browser has nowhere else to keep it; download it as a folder anytime.) Teams choose per project between managed-cloud processing and encryption, with the recovery trade-offs explained first.

There are no feeds, likes, follower counts, or behavioral ads. Mahanu does not sell your data.

Project owners can search the Support & Resources directory for a grant, discount, or service. Sponsors do not receive project work or member identities. They cannot buy influence. See Sponsorship.

Starter projects & portfolios

Mahanu launches with clonable starters — real, lived-in projects you make yours. Pick the one that matches your life, change one thing, invite someone — all in the browser, and a project of one counts. Over time a project quietly becomes a portfolio: published work lands on its public page, the "cloned from" credit makes your influence visible, and because the Timeline keeps every draft, your work carries its own provenance: the making, not only the result.

Safety, plainly

Project interiors are invite-only. Only screened cards and work that members publish are public. Each public item has a report button.

Independent accounts are 18+ and require an invite. A parent can create a supervised child account and control its devices, access, privacy mode, and assistant route.

Mahanu does not use private content for behavioral advertising. A visible conduct bot checks conduct inside large projects and projects with children. Ordinary flags go to project owners and, where relevant, guardians. Encrypted projects are supported; their private files remain unreadable to Mahanu. See Safety for parents.
