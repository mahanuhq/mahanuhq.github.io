Mahanu philosophy

This document guides project screening, assistant policy, and public showcases. The project rule does not settle every case. Other documents link here instead of repeating these values.

Ten things we believe in

  1. Truth is shared and updatable. Under the same conditions, anyone should be able to check a claim and get the same result. As new evidence appears, truth must be tested and updated. It helps us understand our choices and shape our future. Truth reaches across people but remains open to change; a wide-space, narrow-time artifact.

  2. Beauty is personal and enduring. The same work can move people in different ways. Beauty draws us toward what we study and create, and sustains us along the way. True beauty continues to nourish us after the moment of encounter. It is intimate in its reach but lasting in time; a narrow-space, wide-time artifact.

  3. Hard work and perseverance improve the odds. Direction and speed matter more than the starting point. It is never too late to change course. Truth guides the work, and beauty sustains it. Michael Faraday began as a bookbinder’s apprentice with little formal education, learning through books, lectures, and years of experimentation. Vincent van Gogh became an artist at twenty-seven, then spent a decade studying perspective, anatomy, colour, and line.

  4. Learning compounds when shared. An ordinary family today eats better, heals faster, and hears more music than the royalty of just a century ago—because millions of people learned, shared what they knew, and built on one another’s work. This requires the generosity to help others and the humility to learn from them and accept their help. Helping strangers is not charity; it is self-interest with a long horizon.

  5. Projects must be positive-sum. A project passes when everyone it affects can benefit. It fails this test if one person's gain requires another's loss (zero-sum), one loss can cause ruin (high-stakes), or individual gains destroy a shared resource (tragedy of commons). Competition can support practice. Gambling does not qualify.

  6. Culture and enforcement follow different standards. Our cultural test asks whether a project contributes truth or beauty. Enforcement follows the binding rules in our Acceptable Use Policy. Automated systems do not judge beauty, artistic merit, or political opinion. Decisions about hosted services are governed by our Reporting & Appeals Policy. Culture matters, but our judgments remain open to challenge and correction.

  7. Screen a project's purpose under the AUP. Chemistry, martial arts, persuasion, lockpicking, and security research can each be valid study. The AUP defines legal limits and enforcement. Mahanu does not rate the quality of members' work. Listing is a hosted-service decision.

  8. Hosts decide unclear listing cases. In the end, hosts decide what runs on their resources. The MIT license allows others to run the full stack, including the server, under different listing rules.

  9. Safety controls are structural. Project interiors are invite-only. Public surfaces are small and screened. The assistant declines harmful requests.

  10. The work must outlive Mahanu. Project content uses open-format files. Members keep work that reached a desktop vault, a self-hosted copy, or a downloaded folder if Mahanu closes.

Where this is applied

This applies in three places: directory card screening, the assistant's refusal policy, and review of future public surfaces such as showcases, competitions, and sponsorships. Each should link here instead of repeating the rules.
