Mahanu philosophy

This page guides project screening, assistant policy, and future public showcases. It does not replace the Acceptable Use Policy, which controls hosted-service enforcement.

Things we believe in

  1. Truth is shared and updatable. A claim should be checkable under the same conditions by anyone. Better evidence should change our view. Truth helps us understand our choices and shape our future.
  2. Beauty is personal and lasting. The same work may move different people differently. Beauty draws us and sustains us along the way. True beauty continues to give us joy long after the moment of encounter.
  3. Direction and persistence matter. The current point does not decide the end. Practice improves the odds, and it is not too late to change course. Michael Faraday began as a bookbinder’s apprentice with little formal education, learning through books, lectures, and years of experimentation. Vincent van Gogh became an artist at twenty-seven, then spent a decade studying perspective, anatomy, colour, and line.
  4. Learning compounds when shared. Even ordinary people today eat better, heal better, hear more music than even royalty from just a few centuries ago. We benefit from knowledge others preserved. Extending the chain requires generosity to help and humility to accept help. But helping strangers is not charity; it is self-interest with a long horizon.
  5. Projects should be positive-sum. A project should create value without requiring another person's loss (zero-sum) or ruin (high-stakes) or destroying a shared resource (tragedy of commons). Competition may support practice; gambling does not qualify.
  6. Culture and enforcement are different. Mahanu may choose what it celebrates. Enforcement follows published rules, evidence, and appeal—not taste or viewpoint.
  7. Purpose is judged separately from quality. Chemistry, martial arts, persuasion, lockpicking, and security research may all be valid pursuits. The AUP sets legal and safety limits. Mahanu does not grade the work.
  8. Hosts decide what they host. Mahanu decides what runs on its service. Because the stack is MIT-licensed, another host may make different listing choices.
  9. Safety should come from clear boundaries. Membership, public visibility, publishing, permissions, and reporting should be explicit.
  10. The work should outlive Mahanu. Members should be able to keep readable files, independent replicas, and the open-source software.

These values apply where Mahanu exercises judgment. They do not authorize routine reading of local projects or turn culture into a hidden ranking system.
