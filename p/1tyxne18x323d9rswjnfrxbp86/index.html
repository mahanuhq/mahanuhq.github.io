Snakes of India

A draft project — the card and first plan; working docs start with the project.

India loses more people to snakebite than any country on earth, and most of the harm is downstream of two things: not knowing which snake it was, and doing the wrong thing in the first hour. Meanwhile the snake usually dies too, almost always a harmless one killed on suspicion.

This project is the household version of what a rescuer knows: identification good enough to be calm, a first-aid protocol that matches what hospitals actually do, and a list of what folklore recommends that will get someone killed.

First plan

0 of 7 done
  • Identification: start with the four that matter most in this region, then the harmless ones most often mistaken for them
  • A field card per species — pattern, head shape, behaviour, where it's likely to be, and what it is not
  • Bite protocol, written for a panicking person: immobilise, keep the limb below the heart, go to a hospital with antivenom, note the time
  • The do-nots, spelled out — no tourniquet, no cutting, no sucking, no ice, no chasing the snake for a photo
  • Where the antivenom actually is: nearest hospitals that stock it, with distances and numbers, checked twice a year
  • Rescuer contacts by area, and what to do while waiting for one
  • House and garden: what attracts them, what a snake-proofed store room looks like, monsoon precautions

Open questions

  • Everything here has to be checked against current medical guidance rather than assembled from the internet. Whose protocol do we treat as canonical?
  • Regional species lists differ a lot. Do we scope to Karnataka and the Ghats, or all of India and stay shallow?

Status

Draft — nothing in this project is advice until a doctor and a rescuer have read it.
