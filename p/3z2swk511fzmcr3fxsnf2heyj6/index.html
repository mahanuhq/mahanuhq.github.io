Mirror for Music

A draft project — the card and first plan; working docs start with the project.

A student leaves a lesson with a teacher's correction in their ear and loses it by Tuesday. This project keeps the reference: the reference rendition and the student's, side by side, and the difference between them made visible. Most of what a teacher corrects in the first five minutes of a class — rushing, dragging, sitting under the pitch, swallowing the ends of phrases — is measurable from the audio, so the student can catch it during the week instead of paying class time for it.

The point is not a score. It's a practice loop that closes on its own.

First plan

0 of 6 done
  • Capture: one recording per phrase, reference then student, tagged with lesson date and what was being worked on
  • Time domain — onset timing against the reference's take: where the student rushed, where they dragged, and by how much
  • Frequency domain — pitch track per note: flat, sharp, and how long the drift took to correct
  • Dynamics — loudness envelope: the phrase that starts strong and dies, the ornament that disappears
  • A practice card per week: the three things worth fixing, in the order that will make the biggest difference
  • Rule: the analysis suggests, the teacher decides — a number never overrules the person in the room

Open questions

  • How much of this is genuinely automatic, and how much needs the teacher to mark the reference take first?
  • Gharana and school differ on what counts as correct timing — whose reference is the reference?
  • Does a student practising against a graph start playing to the graph? Worth watching for.

Status

Draft — the analysis is the easy half; the hard half is a weekly card a twelve-year-old will actually read.
