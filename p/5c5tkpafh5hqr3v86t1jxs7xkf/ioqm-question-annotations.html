IOQM sample questions, prerequisites and difficulty

Audience: a CBSE Class 8 student using the upcoming IOQM as a sampler and building toward the main attempt next year.

How to read this

Difficulty is learner-relative, not the official mark value:

  • 1 - Foundation: mostly Class 8 knowledge; aim to solve quickly.
  • 2 - Approachable: one standard olympiad idea beyond routine schoolwork.
  • 3 - Core IOQM: several linked observations or a less familiar technique.
  • 4 - Hard: substantial ingenuity or advanced olympiad fluency; study after attempting.
  • 5 - Very hard: unusually deep at the learner's current stage; record it for the year-long plan rather than forcing it before the sampler.

Each question is reproduced as selectable text, with mathematical expressions typeset from LaTeX. The statements were checked against the source PDFs after conversion. AoPS discussion links go to the year collection listed in resources.md; AoPS groups that year's problem discussions on that page. JPSaha links are included only when the local handout is directly relevant to the technique.

Suggested use before the sampler IOQM

  1. Solve representative difficulty 1 questions and enough difficulty 2 questions to learn the paper's style.
  2. Sit one full paper under exam conditions. Practise answer-sheet discipline, time allocation and moving on without frustration.
  3. Try difficulty 3 questions as stretch problems, but do not cram unfamiliar techniques solely to raise this year's score.
  4. Browse difficulty 4-5 questions to discover appealing subjects. Save serious study of them for the year-long runway.
  5. After the exam, record each question as solved, nearly solved, understood after reading, or not yet understood. That diagnostic matters more than the score.

Use after the sampler, toward next year's IOQM

  1. Revisit every unsolved difficulty 1-2 question until the solution can be reproduced independently.
  2. Build subject foundations, then work through difficulty 3 questions without time pressure.
  3. Add difficulty 4 questions gradually in the learner's strongest two areas.
  4. Return to difficulty 5 questions late in the cycle as enrichment and RMO preparation; they are destinations, not immediate prerequisites.
  5. Repeat past papers periodically so progress is measured in genuinely solved problems rather than material merely read.
