IOQM syllabus and learning map for a CBSE Class 8 student

This map connects the mathematical olympiad syllabus to a practical IOQM progression for the learner.

How to interpret the labels

  • IOQM has no rigid chapter-by-chapter syllabus; problems may combine elementary ideas in unfamiliar ways.
  • Its four major areas are number theory, algebra, combinatorics and geometry.
  • CBSE introduction means the earliest class where a closely related school topic normally appears. It does not mean CBSE teaches the olympiad technique at that grade.
  • Difficulty is calibrated for this learner: a CBSE Class 8 student using the upcoming IOQM as a sampler and preparing seriously for next year's IOQM.
Difficulty Meaning
1 Already in, or close to, Class 8 mathematics
2 Accessible extension of school mathematics
3 Core IOQM technique needing deliberate practice
4 Advanced IOQM/RMO material; learn selectively
5 Long-run IOQM stretch and RMO/INMO enrichment

Two-horizon preparation plan

The upcoming IOQM is a diagnostic and exam-experience run. Its score should not drive last-minute cramming or narrow the year-long curriculum.

Before the sampler - orientation, not completion

  • Learn the exam format, answer-entry rules and a sensible skip/return habit.
  • Refresh divisibility, remainders, algebraic identities, basic counting, angles, similarity, Pythagoras and area ratios.
  • Attempt one complete paper under timed conditions and review it carefully.
  • Enter the exam rested. Use it to observe which questions feel inviting, which techniques are missing and how time pressure changes decisions.

Immediately after the sampler - build the diagnostic

  • Classify every encountered question as solved, nearly solved, understood after reading, or not yet understood.
  • Record whether the obstacle was knowledge, idea generation, algebraic execution, diagram reading, time management or answer-entry accuracy.
  • Choose the weakest foundational area and the strongest motivating area for the first study block.

Foundation block - roughly the first 3-4 months

  • Divisibility, prime factorisation, HCF/LCM and divisor counting
  • Remainders, last digits and basic modular arithmetic
  • Algebraic identities, linear equations and elementary polynomials
  • Basic counting, permutations by cases and pigeonhole principle
  • Angles, triangle inequality, similarity, Pythagoras and area ratios

Aim for independent solutions to difficulty 1-2 past questions. Keep proof writing and explanation in the routine even though IOQM itself uses numerical answers.

IOQM core block - roughly the middle 4-5 months

  • Linear Diophantine equations
  • Quadratics, discriminants, remainder/factor theorem and Vieta
  • Counting complements, simple inclusion-exclusion and elementary colourings
  • Circle basics, cyclic quadrilaterals, tangents and power of a point
  • Coordinates and functional equations based on simple substitutions

Work mainly on difficulty 3 questions, with periodic mixed sets and one timed past paper every few weeks.

Stretch and exam block - final 3-4 months

  • Chinese remainder theorem, Fermat/Euler and harder Diophantine equations
  • Recurrences, invariants, games and graph colourings
  • Ceva, Menelaus, triangle centres and advanced circle geometry
  • Advanced inequalities, irreducibility and generating functions

Select difficulty 4 material according to strengths and interest. Difficulty 5 problems remain valuable enrichment, but are not required before the core is secure. Increase timed-paper frequency near the exam while continuing careful untimed solution review.

Reference books

These are references, not a short-term reading list.

  • Broad: Problem Primer for Olympiads; Challenge and Thrill of Pre-College Mathematics; An Excursion in Mathematics; Arthur Engel's Problem Solving Strategies; Mathematical Circles.
  • Algebra: Hall and Knight's Higher Algebra; Barnard and Child's Higher Algebra; Barbeau's Polynomials; Venkatachala's books on functional equations and inequalities.
  • Geometry: Coxeter and Greitzer's Geometry Revisited; Sharygin's Problems in Plane Geometry; Loney's books on plane trigonometry and coordinate geometry.
  • Combinatorics: Brualdi's Introductory Combinatorics; Lovasz-Pelikan-Vesztergombi's Discrete Mathematics; Sane's Combinatorial Techniques; Muralidharan's Combinatorics for Mathematical Olympiad.
  • Number theory: Burton's Elementary Number Theory; Niven-Zuckerman-Montgomery's An Introduction to the Theory of Numbers.
