How ideas get checked

Contributions and published briefs have different requirements.

To contribute: send one paragraph. It needs no citation, proposal, or solution.

To enter a brief: meet every requirement below. Editors assemble this from contributions. A contributor does not need to provide it all.

After you send it

It gets read, and filed under the question it speaks to, alongside whatever contradicts it. You can choose how you are quoted, and you choose how you are credited: name, first name, a role ("a teacher in Kalaburagi"), or none. What changed, and what is still disputed, gets published. If it isn't used, ask why and you get an answer.

The bar for a brief

A proposal is ready only with:

  1. a precise problem, and who it affects;
  2. why it would work — the causal step, not a slogan;
  3. evidence for it and against it;
  4. its effects on equity, rights, privacy, and accessibility;
  5. operational feasibility, security, cost, and legality;
  6. alternatives, how it could fail, and what would trigger reversing it;
  7. an outcome that can be measured, and someone who'd own it;
  8. an answer to the strongest objection anyone raised.

What can't clear this stays on the site as an open question. Most of the value here is likely to be in the well-posed questions.

Review lenses

A reviewer says which lens they used, so a reader knows what was checked and what wasn't:

sources and factual accuracy · bias and fallacy · precedent, Indian and international, including failures · constitutional, statutory, and administrative law · rights, ethics, privacy, due process · caste, class, gender, disability, language, geography, digital access · assessment validity and effect on what gets taught · institutional incentives and capture · security, fraud, abuse · feasibility, cost, capacity, transition · second-order effects and Goodhart's law · measurability, auditability, reversibility, accountability.

Usefulness and accuracy

An objection can be useful even when it is wrong. Published reviews state both its usefulness and accuracy.
