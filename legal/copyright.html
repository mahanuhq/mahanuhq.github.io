Copyright & Intellectual Property Policy

Pre-launch draft · not yet in effect. Before launch, a lawyer must complete the notice address, local legal steps, and reply procedure. This policy supports the Terms of Service. It does not give Mahanu ownership of user content.

1. Rights in content

Members keep the rights they hold in their work. They must have permission to add material, share it with project members, or publish it. Being in the same project does not give an owner permission to publish another member’s work.

Mahanu receives only the limited service and publication permissions described in the Terms. Mahanu’s name, logos, and other brand assets are not granted for reuse except under any separate published brand rules. The software is governed by its MIT license, not this policy.

A project may choose a licence for its writing and art. Its card can show a short label and link to the full terms. Mahanu records and displays that choice but does not set, own, or override it. Mahanu’s own project content terms are at /LICENSE.html.

2. Copyright notices

For material available on a Mahanu public card or public page, send a copyright notice to copyright@mahanu.com with:

  1. identification of the copyrighted work;
  2. the exact URL or enough information to locate the challenged material;
  3. your contact information;
  4. a good-faith statement that the use is not authorised; and
  5. any statements or signature required by the applicable jurisdiction.

We assess complete notices under applicable law, may ask for more information, and may restrict or remove public material while reviewing. We do not disclose private project content merely because a notice asks for it.

3. Responses and repeat misuse

Where appropriate and lawful, the affected project receives the notice and may reply. The final process must include a valid counter-notice where required. It must explain when content may return. If Mahanu uses the U.S. safe-harbour process, it must name a registered agent. Repeated or serious infringement may lead to action under the Acceptable Use Policy.

Use Reporting & Appeals for non-copyright safety, privacy, or access concerns.
